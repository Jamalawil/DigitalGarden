# The Acoustic Ecology of Disappearing Soundscapes

Bernie Krause has been recording natural soundscapes for fifty years. The arc of his archive is devastating: the same locations recorded across decades reveal the progressive hollowing out of the acoustic environment. Species vanish from soundscapes years before they appear in population counts.

His framework divides environmental sound into three categories: **biophony** (sound made by living organisms), **geophony** (sound from non-biological sources — wind, water, thunder), and **anthropophony** (sound made by humans). Healthy ecosystems produce dense, layered biophony across the frequency spectrum, each species occupying its own acoustic niche.

What we are losing is the density. The layers thin. The silences between calls grow. The soundscape becomes legible as a graph of decline.

This matters for journalism because it offers a way to represent ecological loss that does not depend on statistics or scientific testimony. You can *hear* it. The before and after recordings are the evidence.

Krause's book *The Great Animal Orchestra* is essential. His archive at the Wild Sanctuary is a primary source for anyone working on the Eremocene.
