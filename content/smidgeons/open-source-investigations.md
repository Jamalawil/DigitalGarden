# Open Source Investigations: The Methodological Revolution

Bellingcat has demonstrated — repeatedly, at scale — that open source intelligence techniques can produce investigative journalism that traditional reporting methods cannot. Satellite imagery, social media geolocation, flight tracking, corporate registry data: the digital footprint of events is often more complete and more verifiable than eyewitness testimony.

The implications for investigative journalism are significant and underexplored in most editorial discussions.

**Verification without access.** OSINT methods allow journalists to verify events in locations where direct reporting access is impossible. This changes the geography of accountability journalism.

**Reproducible evidence.** Unlike source-based journalism — where the reader must trust the journalist's account of what a source said — OSINT journalism produces evidence chains that other journalists and members of the public can verify independently. The methodology is transparent.

**New skill requirements.** The tools are learnable but not trivial. Journalists who want to work in this way need to invest in technical skills that were not historically part of the craft.

The Bellingcat training materials are the best introduction to the methodology for working journalists.
