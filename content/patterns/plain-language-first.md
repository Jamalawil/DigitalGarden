# Plain Language First

Write the simplest, clearest sentence that communicates your meaning. Then ask: does the subject require more complexity? If not, keep it plain.

## The Pattern

1. Draft in plain language
2. Identify places where simplification loses precision
3. Add complexity only where precision requires it
4. Never add complexity for style

## Examples

**Instead of**: "The phenomenon of acoustic isolation, characterized by the reduction in biodiversity of soundscapes..."

**Write**: "Soundscapes are going quiet as species disappear."

## When to Break It

Scientific or legal writing sometimes requires technical precision that plain language cannot provide. But even then, introduce technical terms by first stating the plain-language equivalent.
