# Show the Data, Then Tell the Story

In data journalism there is a persistent temptation to lead with the conclusion — the percentage, the ranking, the comparison — and let the reader fill in the human reality. This is backwards.

## The Pattern

**Anchor with a specific case first.** The number means nothing without a face. Begin with one person, one place, one event that concretises what the data represents. The statistics gain power from the specific, not the other way around.

**Present the data on its own terms.** After the anchor, give the reader the actual numbers. Not the journalist's interpretation of the numbers, but the numbers themselves — presented clearly enough that a careful reader could check the journalist's conclusions.

**Then draw the conclusion.** With the specific case in mind and the data laid out, the pattern can be named. The reader has enough information to verify the claim rather than simply accept it.

## Why This Order Matters

Readers process abstract claims differently from concrete ones. Abstract percentage gains or losses slide through attention without landing. A specific family, business, or street creates a mental image the reader carries through the rest of the piece.

The pattern also serves transparency. Showing the data before asserting the conclusion signals that the conclusion follows from evidence that the reader can examine. It is the difference between "trust me" and "here is what I found."

## Common Failures

**The buried lede.** The data is powerful, the conclusion is buried in paragraph eleven, and readers who do not finish never understand what the data shows.

**The overwhelming table.** Presenting all the data at once — before the reader has a reason to care — produces attention collapse. The data that appears before the story has established stakes will not be processed.

**The unsupported claim.** Drawing a conclusion that goes beyond what the data can support, even when the intuition is correct, undermines the journalism. Mark the gap between what the data shows and what you believe.
