# The Garden and the Stream: A Different Web for Knowledge

Delivered at the Knowledge Management Summit, February 2025.

Mike Caulfield's distinction between the Garden and the Stream is one of the most useful frameworks I have encountered for thinking about how the web should work versus how it does.

The Stream — social media, feeds, breaking news — is temporal and ephemeral. Content exists in relation to what came before and what will come after. Its value is partly its currency. A tweet about something that happened last week is almost worthless.

The Garden is topological. Content exists in relation to other content, not to time. A note about acoustic ecology links to notes about the Eremocene, about documentary methodology, about Bernie Krause. Its value grows as connections accumulate. Age is not a defect.

This talk explores what it would mean to rebuild journalism as a Garden rather than a Stream — what that would demand of reporters, editors, and readers — and whether the digital garden movement offers a practical path towards it.
