# Building MARKA: Notes from the Beginning

I started MARKA without a fully formed plan. This is probably a mistake. It is also, I think, the only honest way to begin something like this.

MARKA is a media project. More specifically, it is an attempt to do investigative journalism in communities that most investigative journalism ignores — not because those communities are unimportant, but because the business models that fund investigative journalism have not historically found them worth serving.

## The Gap I Am Trying to Close

Investigative journalism is expensive. It requires time, legal support, records requests, travel, and the kind of institutional backing that allows reporters to spend months on a single story. These resources flow to where the audience is, and the audience that advertisers and foundations find most valuable is not evenly distributed.

The communities I am most interested in covering — migrant communities, informal workers, people navigating systems designed without them in mind — are communities that existing investigative journalism reaches poorly.

This is not a critique of the journalists working in mainstream investigative outlets. It is a structural observation. The economics of investigative journalism produce a coverage gap, and MARKA is my attempt to close a small part of it.

## What I Know and What I Do Not

I know the journalism I want to do. I have done versions of it throughout my career, in formats constrained by the institutions I worked within. I have a clear sense of the stories that need telling and the communities they are about.

I do not fully know the business model. I have hypotheses — a combination of grants, membership, and commissioned work — but these are untested at the scale I am imagining. This garden includes my working notes on the model as I test it.

I do not know how to build a team yet. Investigative journalism requires editors, fact-checkers, lawyers, visual journalists. I am currently one person. This will have to change.

## Why Document This Publicly?

There are arguments against building in public. It exposes uncertainty. It may alert the institutions you are investigating that something is coming. It creates commitments you may not be able to keep.

But I think the arguments for it are stronger. If MARKA works, the lessons should be shareable — not every media project needs to make the same mistakes. If it fails, the documentation of failure is at least honest and may be useful to someone.

And there is something about working through ideas in public that improves the thinking. This note is already clearer than the version I had in my head before I started writing it.

## The Name

MARKA is a word that crosses several languages I care about. It means mark, sign, brand — the trace that something leaves. It is what a story does when it works: it marks the reader. They are not the same after having read it.

That is the ambition.
